Reconcile the booking before checking each recipient

When Airbnb pays part of a booking to a co-host, reconcile the booking to both allocations, then match each allocation to its own receipt evidence. The owner's bank deposit alone cannot explain the full settlement. Keep the reservation reference, platform deductions, agreed split, payout references and unresolved amounts together. This is a practical approach to Airbnb co-host payout reconciliation for a Bali villa's records, rather than a method for deciding who legally earns the booking income.

Who this applies to

This guide is for Bali villa owners and managers preparing records where an Airbnb home reservation has a co-host payout. It is particularly useful when an overseas owner receives one amount and a local operator receives another. It also helps the person preparing an owner statement explain a smaller bank receipt without describing the difference as missing money.

Check the account and listing actually involved. Airbnb identifies location-based restrictions in its regional limitations for co-host payouts. This guide does not establish that a particular arrangement is eligible. It also does not cover setting up payouts or selecting an Indonesian tax treatment.

Separate the payout instruction from the accounting conclusion

Airbnb's co-host payout guidance describes cleaning-fee, percentage and fixed-amount options. A payout instruction describes the movement of money; your bookkeeping file still needs the underlying agreement and supporting records. Do not infer ownership of income, an expense classification or an outstanding invoice solely from the recipient's name.

Use three separate questions. What did the platform allocate? What did each recipient actually receive? What does the agreement say the payment settles? The first two support a cash reconciliation. The third helps the responsible accountant assess the appropriate records and treatment. Keeping these questions separate prevents an apparently neat spreadsheet from concealing an unsupported assumption.

Also distinguish a co-host payment from routing money between payout methods. Airbnb documents those instructions separately in its split-payout guidance. Name the actual arrangement in the reconciliation register rather than using “split” as a catch-all description. A destination bank account is not, by itself, an explanation of the commercial relationship.

Build one booking-level evidence register

Start with one reservation reference and connect every settlement component to it. Record the property, operating entity, stay dates, reporting period, currency, platform amount, deductions, co-host allocation and owner allocation. Give each payout its own reference and status. Attach the original report rather than repeatedly replacing a spreadsheet total with the latest number.

The register should distinguish an expected allocation from a confirmed receipt. Use simple statuses such as allocated, released, receipt confirmed and unresolved. These are suggested internal labels, not Airbnb interface labels. Keep the person responsible for resolving each open item visible, together with the question they need to answer.

Collect only the evidence needed for that question. A recipient may provide a redacted transaction confirmation showing the relevant reference, date, currency and amount. Avoid requesting unrelated personal transactions. If evidence cannot be shared yet, retain the item as unconfirmed and state exactly what remains missing.

Airbnb says listing owners cannot access a co-host's transaction history. Its explanation of co-host earnings visibility is an important reason to agree an evidence handover in advance. Seeing an allocation in the host's account is not the same as independently confirming its arrival in another person's bank.

Explain receipts that fall into different months

Now assume the example allocation is visible on 31 October, the owner confirms receipt on 2 November and the co-host confirms receipt on 4 November. These dates illustrate a record trail, not expected processing times or a payment promise.

At the October checkpoint, IDR 10,000,000 remains unconfirmed as received in this illustrative schedule. After the owner's confirmation, IDR 8,000,000 is matched and IDR 2,000,000 remains unconfirmed. After both confirmations, the unconfirmed amount is zero. Preserve the platform release dates separately from these receipt dates.

Do not change the stay dates to make the cash schedule balance. Keep the reservation period, platform activity date and bank receipt date in separate fields. Let the accountant decide the appropriate reporting treatment. The schedule's job is to explain the evidence and timing, including any amount whose destination is still unconfirmed.

A decision aid for differences

  • The allocation totals agree, but a bank receipt is missing: retain the payout reference and ask for receipt confirmation. Do not invent a receipt date.
  • The two allocations do not add to the reported settlement: inspect the selected reservation, currency, deductions and applicable instruction before changing the records.
  • The cash matches, but an invoice also appears unpaid: establish whether the platform payment settles that invoice or a different obligation. Link the documents before considering another transfer.
  • A later adjustment appears: link it to the original booking and actual adjustment record. Do not automatically apply the original split to a refund or infer the platform's recovery method.
  • The parties disagree about what the allocation covers: mark the commercial question unresolved and refer to the agreement. Keep the confirmed cash trail intact.

What owners get wrong

Counting only the owner's receipt. This leaves the other allocation unexplained. Correct it by building the booking-level bridge first, then matching the separate destinations. Do not turn the unexplained difference into a balancing expense.

Paying the same obligation again. A co-host may send an invoice while the platform has already transferred an amount connected to that work. Correct it by documenting what each payment settles. An invoice number, reservation reference and recipient confirmation are more useful than assuming all similar amounts are duplicates.

Combining different parties' income in one report. A cash register covering the booking can help explain settlement, but it is not permission to combine the owner's and manager's accounts. Correct it by identifying the reporting entity and keeping the allocation schedule distinct from its financial statements.

A month-end handover checklist

  1. Identify the reservation, villa, reporting entity, currency and period.
  2. Save the original platform breakdown and applicable allocation instruction.
  3. Connect each deduction and allocation without counting any component twice.
  4. Match each available payout reference to relevant receipt evidence.
  5. List unconfirmed receipts and commercial questions with an owner and next action.
  6. Link invoices, adjustments and later confirmations to the same reservation record.
  7. Check the report includes the intended listings and date range.
  8. Retain the completed version and the original supporting files.

Airbnb's earnings-download guidance describes reports and exports with filters and earnings details. Use the options actually available in the relevant account, and record the report period. Avoid hard-coding a menu sequence into your internal process because interfaces and available options may differ.

A useful handover note explains the remaining exception precisely: “Co-host allocation recorded; receipt evidence requested; no additional payment approved.” This lets the next person continue the work without confusing missing documentation with missing cash.

Before closing the file, ask someone to follow one reservation from the original report to the register and then to the available receipt evidence. They should be able to identify which figures are confirmed, which are expected and which question remains open without relying on a verbal explanation. Keep the report filters and export date alongside the file so a later reviewer can reproduce that check.

Agree the records before the next close

Decide who obtains the host report, who confirms the co-host receipt and who reviews unresolved items. Keep commercial interpretation with the appropriately responsible person. A bookkeeper should not have to reconstruct an agreement from bank descriptions after each stay.

Frequently asked questions

Is the owner's bank receipt the full booking amount?

Not necessarily. For the illustrated arrangement, it is one part of the settlement after the stated deduction and co-host allocation. Reconcile the complete booking breakdown before interpreting an individual deposit.

Can the owner see the co-host's transaction history?

Airbnb says listing owners cannot access that history. Agree an appropriate confirmation process with the co-host and distinguish the visible allocation from evidence that the recipient actually received it.

Does a co-host allocation automatically settle a management invoice?

No conclusion should be drawn from the amount alone. Check the agreement, invoice and reservation reference, then document whether the transfer settles that obligation. Hold a further payment decision until the connection is clear.

What if the two receipts arrive in different months?

Keep their actual receipt dates and the original booking reference. Show any unconfirmed balance in the cash schedule and let the accountant assess the appropriate reporting treatment separately.

Does this establish the correct Indonesian tax treatment?

No. The register explains settlement evidence. It does not decide taxable income, deductibility or filing obligations. Those questions require the actual facts and appropriately qualified advice.
